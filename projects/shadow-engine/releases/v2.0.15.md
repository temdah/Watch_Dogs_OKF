# Failure and recovered ownership

The expanded shadow-result path leaked native acquisitions when accepted results
were not retired at the owning consumer boundary. The corrected experiment
consumed and released accepted results at the native frame-graph pre-finalizer.
Every rejected non-null result also released its corresponding acquisition.
Equal pointer values did not mean duplicate acquisitions could be ignored.

Producer-owned cycle tracking prevented a later builder from resetting valid
in-flight result ownership. Producer return was not completion. The detailed
[external-result lifecycle](../architecture/external-slice-results.md) owns the
native addresses, publication rules and rejected placements.

# Observed result and limits

The Global intersection/dense-bridge run with 30 maps and B4=21/A8=4 recorded
120,106 completed/consumed results, balanced releases, and zero recorded stale,
late, rejected, release-failure or face-clamp events. The visual report was stable.
The experimental ASI SHA256 was
`8B749E574CFA35DDB5877A398D85522F94CFF357F133D8EC8050A01EBD4321D8`.

This supports that ownership correction in the recorded Global run. It does not
prove absence of every leak, dense-scene flicker or performance problem. Four
profiles had offline byte checks; fresh runtime proof outside Global and the
Shev/A4EE hotfix test were not established. Full-capacity overflow and all-scene
visual correctness were not established by this clean control.
